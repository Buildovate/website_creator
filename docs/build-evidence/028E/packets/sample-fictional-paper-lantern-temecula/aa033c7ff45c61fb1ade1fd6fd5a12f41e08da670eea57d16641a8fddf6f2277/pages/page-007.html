COMMON QUESTIONS

Good questions deserve clear answers.

Understand your roof, compare your next steps and bring your questions to the conversation.

Repair or replace

Services

How do I know whether to repair or replace my roof?

Repair often makes sense when damage is limited to one area and the roof is under about 15 years old. Replacement is worth discussing when leaks appear in several places, shingles curl across the roof or repairs keep returning. Roof age, decking and the covering all matter. Describe what you’re seeing and we’ll help you compare the next steps.

What warning signs should I look for?

Look for water stains on ceilings, shingles that have lifted or gone missing, cracked tiles and debris collecting where roof sections meet. Granules gathering in gutters can also signal wear. Check from the ground and photograph indoor stains; leave climbing to a roofer. Tell us where the problem appears and whether it changes after rain.

Cost and timing

Planning & price

How long does roofing work usually take?

Most straightforward repairs take about a day; a full replacement often takes two to four days. Roof size, steep slopes, layers to remove and damaged decking can extend the work, and wet or windy weather can interrupt it. Delivery, permits and scheduling happen before installation. Share your roof type and priorities so we can talk through a practical timeline.

What drives the cost of a roofing project?

Roof size and pitch affect the amount of material and the time needed to work safely. The roof covering, layers to remove, flashing, ventilation, decking damage and permit requirements also shape the estimate. Access around the house can add handling time. Ask for these items in writing so you can compare the same scope across estimates.

How should I prepare for roof work?

Clear the driveway for deliveries and debris removal, move patio furniture away from the roof edge and keep children and pets clear of the work area. Roofing can be noisy, and vibration may disturb items on shelves or walls. Discuss parking, access, cleanup and your daily routine with the crew before the start date is agreed.

Keep your roof healthy

Roof care

How can I keep small roof issues from becoming bigger ones?

Keep gutters and downspouts clear, watch for branches rubbing the roof and check from the ground after strong wind or heavy rain. Inside, note new ceiling stains or damp areas in the attic. Avoid walking on tiles or lifting shingles yourself. Share photographs of changes so a roofer can identify whether maintenance or a closer inspection is appropriate.
