03 / THE EXISTING ROOF

Start with the roof you have

A roof is more than the finish you see from the street. Before comparing new materials, take a little time to describe what is already there.

Think of the covering, its edges and the places where another part of the house meets it. Shingles or tiles are visible; the layers and connections beneath them may not be. A photograph can introduce the conversation, but it cannot show the full condition of that assembly.

Gather any paperwork you already hold: a previous scope, material information or notes about an addition. Missing records are useful to acknowledge too. You do not need to fill the gaps with guesses.

  • Name the covering if you know it. Otherwise, share a clear photo from a safe position.
  • Describe where a newer porch, room or roof section meets the older house.
  • Separate what you have observed from what someone still needs to inspect.
A question to keep

What do I know about this roof, and what am I still trying to understand?

04 / YOUR OBSERVATIONS

Observe without climbing

Useful notes can begin indoors or from the ground. You do not have to get onto the roof to explain a concern.

Write down where you first noticed a stain, loose material or debris. Record when you saw it and whether its appearance changed. A mark on a ceiling is a starting point for investigation, not proof that water entered directly above it.

Use views you can reach safely. Avoid climbing onto a roof or moving a ladder to chase a photograph. If something presents an immediate safety concern, stay clear of the affected area and seek appropriate local help.

  • Photograph the same visible area again if its appearance changes.
  • Mention nearby trees, a chimney or an addition without assuming it caused the problem.
  • Keep dates and observations together so the sequence is easy to explain.
A question to keep

What changed, where did I notice it, and when did it begin?
