Roof work happens above your home, while life carries on below. A useful conversation starts with how the work could fit around the people, pets and routines inside.

Keep the everyday things in view.

Mention a home office, a regular delivery or a gate that needs to stay clear. Ask which entrances and outdoor spaces you can keep using.

Make room before work begins.

Talk about parking, where materials could go and how cleanup would be handled. Those practical details matter whether or not you use an offer.

THE OFFER AND THE ESTIMATE

Put the roof work
on the same page.

Bring any offer you’re considering into the roofing conversation. Your written estimate should show exactly what it changes. With or without an offer, start with a clear description of the work your home needs.

Ask about my roofing estimate →

  1. Bring the exact offer.

    If you saved an offer, bring its name or code so you and the contractor are discussing the same thing.

  2. Match it to the roof work.

    Ask which part of the proposed repair or replacement it can apply to.

  3. Ask for the revised estimate.

    Have the contractor show how the offer affects the quoted work and total, rather than leaving you to work it out.

  4. Keep one agreed version.

    Before moving ahead, keep the estimate that reflects the scope and any offer the contractor has confirmed.
